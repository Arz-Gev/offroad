# Dev notes

What the code can't tell you: conventions, traps, rejected options, baselines, how to test.

Rules for this file: lasting facts only. No history, no dates, no "before / after" numbers (git and the PRs keep those). Name where a value lives instead of copying it, so a retune doesn't make the note wrong. Earlier agents wrote it: treat every claim as a hint, check it, and fix the note when it is wrong.

## Conventions

- **Vehicle frame** (physics and model): `+x` right, `+y` up, **`-z` forward**. `y = 0` is the ground at static ride height on the stock setup (bigger tyres and lift raise the body: `rideRaise`), `z = 0` mid-wheelbase.
- **Wheel spin** `ω > 0` = rolling forward = rotation about `-x`. Tyre torque on the wheel is `-Fx * Re`.
- **Axle roll** `phi > 0` = right side up = rotation about body `+z`. Axle compression `c` is measured at the springs (`springTrack`), not at the wheels.
- **Steer** `+1` = right. Wheel yaw rotation is `-steer` about `y`.
- **N axles**: wheels `2a` (left) and `2a+1` (right) of axle `a`, front to back.
- **Rapier heightfield**: `heights[ix * (N+1) + iz]`, columns = x, rows = z, centred on the collider. Each cell is split along the diagonal **(x1, z0)–(x0, z1)**. `terrainView.js` and every shader that reads the physics heights use the same split; if they differ, wheels float or sink.
- Rapier scene queries see colliders only after a `world.step()`. Wheel rays exclude the vehicle's own body (this also excludes the wheel side cylinders).

## Physics

- Rapier at **240 Hz** (`H` in `main.js`); each step `Vehicle.step()` casts the ray fans once, then runs **4 substeps** (960 Hz) and applies the averaged forces. Tyre relaxation and the damper terms are stable only because of 960 Hz: raise substeps if you add stiffness.
- **Axle DOF** uses absolute velocities (`vz`, `Om`); body-relative rates are `vz - vMountU`, `Om - rollRateBody`. Unsprung gravity along `up` is cancelled on the Rapier body, because the axle DOF carries it.
- **Force split**: the tyre force along body-up goes into the axle DOF, the rest acts on the chassis at the hub (roll centre at axle height, panhard rod). The roll moment from side force at the patch goes into the axle roll; that is what lets the truck corner at 1 g without tipping.
- **Independent corners** (`Vehicle.cornerSubstep`): one DOF per wheel; `rcHeight` sets the side force's share into the corner (`q = rc / (track/2)`), the rest goes to the body at the patch. An independent car's anti-roll bar is N/m at the wheels, a beam axle's is N·m/rad; `suspcheck.mjs` and the tuning panel count an independent bar as `arb · track²`.
- **Static loads**: lever rule for 2 axles, a rigid body on equal springs for more (`axleShares`).
- **Tyre model** (`tire.js`):
  - Deflection `ux`, `uy` is clamped by the steady-state slip so a spinning wheel can't wind up; a low-speed damper stops parked trucks oscillating.
  - Radial damping (`tireRadialDamping`) grows when aired down and when creeping. The shocks do ~10× more, so it matters mostly for the heavy BTR. `node tools/tirehop.mjs bump|wash [kmh] [psi]`.
  - Load sensitivity: the peak slip angle grows as `Fn^0.35` (`latPeak`), so cornering stiffness ~`Fn^0.65`; without it load transfer cost no grip and weight split and bars barely changed the balance. It is relative to `P.tire.fnRef` (the 33" tyre's load): without that the BTR's tyres lost a quarter of their μ and it couldn't stand on 30°.
- **Contact patch** (`Vehicle.castContact` / `integrateTyre`): 39 rays, ground linear between rays, `TIRE_SUB` samples between rays, force `K·√δ` per angle, `K` from `tireRadialStiffness(psi)`. The tread band is stiff (loaded profile = upper envelope of the raw one under parabolas of curvature `R / (2·TIRE_BELT)`): without it a step edge cut into the tyre and gave big acceleration spikes. Costs ~0.55–0.75 ms per physics step for 4 wheels. `node tools/tiretest.mjs rock|step [kmh] [psi]`.
- **Tilted tyres and hard landings** (`tools/droptest.mjs`; `tools/resettest.mjs` drops the truck at random spots, `ROLL=90` on its side): a tyre squashed onto its rim gives back only `RIM_GIVE_BACK` of the strike; the tilted ground normal is `n_wheelplane·√(1−s²) + axle·s` (exact for a plane; the cheaper approximation let side grip hold a rolled truck up on its tyre shoulders); the outer ray rows add the tread overhang × s, else the downhill edge sank unfelt.
- **Physics ground = drawn ground** (`node tools/groundmatch.mjs`): terrain rays and `surfaceHeight` must agree to fractions of a mm. A boulder's collider is the convex hull of its vertices and the drawn rock is pushed out onto that hull (`props.js addRock`, ~170 ms at load), so tyres don't float over dents. Hull above mesh: median ~0.1 mm, 90 % ~8 mm, 99 % ~29 mm; much more means `addRock` broke.
- **Drivetrain**: open diffs need no special code (the gear row with `-G/4` per wheel already splits equally). Torque reactions: body gets `-(Ie*αe + Tprop_f + Tprop_r)` about `+z`, each axle `+Tprop` (launch roll, axle wrap); sign follows a crank turning clockwise seen from the front.
- **Drive layouts** (`drive.layout`: 'awd' default, 'parttime', 'rwd', 'fwd'; `driveLayout(P)`): centre-diff groups (`axles[i].group`, axles in a group rigidly linked), cam-type LSD axle diffs, centre split `drive.centreSplit` (group 0's share, 0.5 = equal), `drive.centre: 'viscous'` bounds the lock by Nm per rpm of prop slip. With several handbrake axles each needs its own row: one row on the mean let the groups counter-rotate through the centre diff and held nothing. `node tools/difftest.mjs [car]`, `npm run simtest layouts`. In difftest the Lancia with TC off at full throttle on split grip yaws round: expected (its Torsen and coupling spin the dirt wheels too; a driver would lift).
- **Handbrake**: rear brake plus a standstill hold on the transfer output (`brakes.handbrakeHold`, fades out by 2 m/s), so an open centre diff can't let the front pull away. `node tools/hbtest.mjs [deg]`. **2WD** (F): `drivetrain.rwd`, high range only, forces the centre lock off; `wheelMean()` follows the driven wheels.
- **Handling balance** (`tools/yawtest.mjs`): mild understeer (~1.9°/g at the road wheels on dirt; lifting off at the limit tucks the nose in a little and settles) that comes from real parts, not a stability aid: tyre load sensitivity, front weight share, front roll-stiffness share a little above it, steering compliance (`Vehicle.steerCompliance`), rear roll steer (`rollSteer`). Yaw inertia includes the axles. Roll damping matters: at ζ≈0.2 the truck rocked itself onto two wheels.
- **Traction control** (`vehicle.tc`, Y) brakes any wheel faster than the ground under it so open diffs send torque to wheels with grip; full authority below ~30 km/h. Its cap scales with `P.brakes.front` (a fixed cap let eight spinning BTR wheels sit on a 30° ramp forever). Rolling backwards with wheels driven forwards counts as wheelspin, so TC brakes them and the vehicle slides on: gradient tests climb from a run-up, like real ones.
- **Auto upshift** uses `min(wheel speed, ground speed)` (else it upshifts while the wheels spin in the rock garden). Manual-only cars (`P.manualOnly`) get `autoShift`: at full throttle it upshifts only if the next gear still accelerates (else a 30° climb in 1st low shifted at the governor and bogged).
- **Driveline losses** `P.tire.hubDragV` (BTR: hub reductions, eight wheel drives): without it the BTR ran to its governor well past its real top speed.

## Traps already hit

- **Wheel side cylinders must never touch the terrain heightfield.** They are teleported to the hub every step, so ground contact is a huge impulse on the whole truck (10–37 g spikes, the "pogo stick" ride). Heightfields are `GROUP_GROUND`; the cylinders filter it out and only hit rocks, logs and trees.
- **Don't fix understeer with rear roll stiffness.** Moving roll stiffness to the rear once made the rear-heavy truck nearly neutral and it spun on lift-off at speed. The real cause was keyboard steering asking for far too much angle (next item).
- **Keyboard steering at speed is limited by lateral acceleration** (`applyInput`, setting `steerAssist`, `STEER_ASSIST` in `Vehicle.js`): a held key asks for the angle that corners at k × the grip under the truck + c × the front tyres' peak slip angle. Input filter only: flooring the gas at the limit can still spin the truck, by design. The gamepad keeps its own speed curve; touch steering counts as analog. `node tools/steerassist.mjs` (`FULL=1` floors the gas).
- With no one steering the truck wanders slowly under full power (axle wrap + roll steer). Real, but headless runs need room.
- **Teleport stall**: `reset()` zeroes the wheel speeds, which stalls an engine with the converter locked; so it drops the lock-up and sets `startGrace`.
- After `world.step()` Rapier has cleared the solver contacts (`numSolverContacts()` is 0). Read `numContacts()` with `contactDist(j) < 0.01` and `localContactPoint1/2` (pick by `flipped`) instead (`touchPoints` in `colliderView.js`).
- **Static colliders cost even when far or disabled** (~0.12 µs per collider per 240 Hz step); only removal helps, hence `ColliderStream` (32 m chunks, ±2 around the truck).
- `flat` is a reserved GLSL word.
- three r186 removed `PCFSoftShadowMap`; use `PCFShadowMap`. Use `NeutralToneMapping`: ACES made red paint look salmon.
- The Sky shader is very bright: by day bloom needs a high threshold (`bloomThr` in the `environment.js` keys), or everything washes out white.
- Screen-space bump mapping in the terrain shader wrecked the normals and hid all ground shadows. For ground detail use a real normal map.
- Canvas `putImageData` premultiplies alpha and destroys colour where alpha is low. Procedural textures go straight into `DataTexture`s.
- Terrain features narrower than ~2 grid cells (0.5 m) alias into washboard bumps on diagonal trails. Keep features wide.
- Trail junctions: blend the trails' smoothed heights by weight, and compute the end corrections **before** mutating the array (a bug left a 2 m cliff).
- **16 texture units.** The terrain shader already binds 14. Every `SpotLight.map` (cookie) and every shadowed point light adds a sampler to *every* lit program; going over breaks materials on most GPUs ("Trying to use 17 texture units"). That is why searchlights have no cookie and the muzzle flash light casts no shadow.
- **Lights hash**: changing a light's visibility or shadow flag recompiles every lit program. By day the car's lamps are `visible = false` unless switched on (each is a cost); at night (`env.night`) they stay visible at intensity 0, so switching them never recompiles. The day → night switch recompiles once (the parked hitch; a `renderer.compileAsync` prewarm with the lights visible would remove it). The load warms up with one real frame with all lamps on, high beam, frustum culling off, so every material is drawn once in that state (main pass and the head spot's shadow pass) and the cookies are uploaded: the GPU builds a material's pipeline state on its first draw into a target, and without it a lamp switched on where the first view had not looked stalled 130-220 ms (M1 Pro), no new program needed. Keeping the lamps visible by day instead cost 1.5-2 ms a frame on High (the spot loop and its per-vertex cookie coordinates on all the grass and foliage).
- Headlights default off in daytime: each shadow-casting spotlight is a full extra shadow pass for almost no visible light.

## Testing

- **Headless physics**: `npm run simtest [settle|accel|brake|slope|climb|turn|manual|cars|btr|layouts|suspension]`, exit 1 on any FAIL. `VERBOSE=1` prints traces; `TUNE='{json}'` prints only.
- Per-car tools take `CAR=<id>`: `traillap.mjs [kmh] [cornerG] [secs] [analog|digital]`, `handling.mjs [surface]`, `yawtest.mjs`, `steerassist.mjs`, `tiretest.mjs`, `tirehop.mjs`, `droptest.mjs`, `resettest.mjs`. The car as an argument: `suspcheck.mjs [car]` (sag, room to the bump rubber, heave frequency, front roll share), `difftest.mjs [car]`. Defender only: `bumptest.mjs`, `climb.mjs` (`NOTC=1`), `ramptest.mjs`, `retunetest.mjs`, `profile.mjs` (visible body vs colliders: run after moving bodywork or colliders; it shows lower angles than the colliders because shackles and exhaust are left out on purpose). Terrain: `terraintest.mjs`, `junction.mjs`, `trailprofile.mjs` (must list no crests that unload the truck), `mapimage.mjs out.png`.
- **Browser**: `window.game` (`tick(dt)`, `autopilot`, `placeVehicle`, `action(id)`, `timings`, `loadLog`, `env`, `mp`, ...). `shot()` captures only the WebGL canvas; for HUD and menus use `tools/cdp.mjs` (headless Chrome over CDP).
- A hidden pane or background tab throttles `requestAnimationFrame`: FPS readings are meaningless and screenshots stale. Drive with `game.tick` and capture with `tools/shotserver.py` + `shot()` from `tools/browser-snippets.js` (also has the lane and trail drivers).
- **UI**: dispatch `KeyboardEvent`s on `window`; a fake gamepad by overriding `navigator.getGamepads`; touch with Playwright `hasTouch` + CDP `Input.dispatchTouchEvent`. A headless page gets `blur` events, which pause the game: turn `autoPause` off.
- **Dynamic resolution**: `game.updateDynamicResolution(dt, false)` takes fake frame times (a hidden pane can't give real ones).
- **GPU timing** drifts ±30 % with other GPU load (other agents' headless pages, the in-app browser): compare interleaved runs, and end CDP runs on `about:blank`.
- **Graphics benchmarks** (`tools/gfxbench.mjs`, `glprofile.mjs`, `gfxshots.mjs` (fixed views for before / after pictures, `diff=`), `hitchtest.mjs`): the player wants numbers from a visible Chrome window (`size=window` / `SIZE=window`; Chrome started with `--remote-debugging-port` and its own `--user-data-dir`), not headless. WebGL timer queries are useless on the M1 Pro (ANGLE on Metal reports 4–7x the frame): `glprofile` finds costs by switching systems off. On the 120 Hz screen Chrome shows a WebGL game at 60 fps unless the whole frame fits in 8.3 ms (WebGPU keeps more frames in flight and gets 90+); `--disable-gpu-vsync --disable-frame-rate-limit` (plus `--test-type` to hide the warning bar) shows the uncapped rate, `gfxbench raf=<s>` measures it.
- **Sound**: `node tools/enginesound.mjs out.wav` renders the worklet through a fixed script and prints each segment's level; `starttest.mjs [auto|manual] [P|N]` / `startsound.mjs` for the engine start (rpm trace: cranks ~170–280 rpm, flares to ~2100, idle after ~3 s; it's player-tuned, so a drivetrain change that moves this trace changed the start). Audio-model reviews of the WAV were inconsistent: the player's ear decides.

## UI (HUD, menu, input, touch)

- **`main.js`** builds the world and the car, wires settings (`APPLY`), actions (`ACTIONS`) and the menu, and runs the frame (`tick`). Split out of it: `loading.js` (loading screen), `graphics.js` (presets, resolution, dynamic resolution, Auto quality), `placement.js` (locations, teleports, Recover).
- **`BINDINGS` in `input.js` is the single source of truth** for controls (dispatch, HUD hints, key caps, the panel's Controls section). To add one: a binding with an `id`, then a handler in `ACTIONS` in `main.js`. `setCarControls` / `hasControl` drop the controls a car lacks (`vehicle/controls.js`) everywhere.
- Discrete actions fire from the key event (`input.onAction`), not the game loop, so they work while driving frames with `game.tick`. On Cmd keydown the held keys are dropped (macOS sends no keyup for them); the F row except F3 and all Cmd/Ctrl/Alt combos go to the browser.
- **Look** (player's brief): black glass, white ink, icons instead of words where an icon is clear, a sentence of explanation behind an ⓘ only where a name or icon can confuse. Descriptions and icon names open in the one tooltip (`tooltip.js`: hover with a safe triangle so a diagonal move into it doesn't close it, click or tap pins it); no native `title` tooltips. Selection is white on black, never a colour. Colour only where a dashboard uses it: amber = an assist working or a caution, red = warning, green = lamps on, blue = high beam. All icons come from `icons.js` (one 24 px line style); add new ones there.
- **Side panel** (`menu.js`, Esc): sections on an icon rail (`SECTIONS`; a row is data: type, label, `info`, `hot` keys, `show(api)`). It never dims the game and by default pauses it (`menuPause`; off, the game runs behind it like the Tuning panel it grew from). The keyboard keeps driving (the panel drops the focus after a click), a gamepad's d-pad / A / B / LB / RB move through it while the sticks and triggers drive (`Input.uiHandler`). Keys open sections (Tab Tuning, P Places, H Controls); the same key closes. Tuning is a section: `TuningPanel.attach(el)` renders into it, `setOpen` gates its per-frame work. Live switches (lights, 4×4, TC, ABS, engine) go through the same drivetrain calls as their keys, so a refused change still says why.
- **Nothing jumps** (player): a click or a changing value must not move or resize anything else. Numbers that change get fixed-width columns (telemetry, compact cluster, stepper); a row that only works with another setting greys out in place (`enable` in `menu.js`) instead of hiding; pop-ups float over the layout (tooltip, tuning Save as / Export / Import); toasts fold away; the tip has its own place, away from the toasts; scroll areas reserve their scrollbar (`scrollbar-gutter`). Unfolding a tuning section and opening the box editor are the expected exceptions.
- **Pause** is its own state (`setPaused` in `main.js`): opening the panel (`menuPause`; closing it resumes, but only a pause the panel made), the pause button next to fullscreen in the HUD (always there) or the panel's, or the window losing focus (`autoPause`). Driving input or a click on the view (not a drag: that only moves the camera, and the paused view keeps drawing while it does) resumes, also with the panel open. There is no badge in the view: the HUD's pause button turns white.
- **HUD warning lights** are always on screen, for every system that changes how the truck drives: dim = idle, white = on, struck through = switched off, amber flashing = working right now (`tcActive`, `absActive`, `Vehicle.steerLimited`, `revTimer` for arcade reverse). A new driving aid gets a light in `HUD.updateTells` and a line in the Controls section's legend (`LEGEND` in `menu.js`).
- **HUD cost rules**: no layout reads per frame; DOM text/classes written only on change (cache in `hud.c`); bars move with `transform`; the rpm dial redraws only on a ≥ 20 rpm move; the panel refreshes its open section 5 times a second, writing only what changed.
- **Touch** (`touch.js`): pointer capture on every control (multi-finger; sliding off keeps the control); `touch.release()` on pause prevents stuck gas. The device becomes `'touch'` only *after* a tap finishes (switching re-renders the UI and would eat the click).
- Picking a car only marks it (`menu.pending`); its restart button reloads, because module state is set once at startup.

## Cars

How to add one: `src/cars/README.md`. Every car is a file in `src/cars/`, listed in `index.js`; `vehicle/carParams.js` `makeCarParams(id)` builds any of them. Shared code never checks which car it is.

- **Each car must feel like itself** (player): copying the Defender's chassis onto the G-Class and Lancia fixed their ride and was rejected. Ride comfort comes from trail-friendly travel and pressures; character from weight, inertia, engine, steering ratio, bars and tyre grip.
- **Tyre sizes are absolute** (`tyreRadius` / `tyreWidth` in `tire.js`, calibrated on the Defender's 33"), 0.5" steps.
- **Setups** are stored per car (`offroad.tuning.v1.<car>`, the Defender keeps the bare key); `tuning.useCar(id)` runs first in `main.js`. Migrations live in `fill` in `tuning.js` (setup `v`, a car's `suspensionV` drops an older suspension section).
- **Tuning panel with more than two axles**: front / rear readouts are the first and last axle. The panel's BTR top speed and 0–60 are a little slower than the physics on purpose (the estimate includes `hubDragV`).
- **Imported models** (`model/shell.js`): the shell's wheels go into our wheel groups; own tyres don't squash; transmission glass becomes plain alpha (transmission costs a second scene render); `realBlack` fixes the dark cabins. `tools/cutparts.mjs` moves pieces inside a box into their own node (steering wheels, lamp glass, driving lamps). `physics.archTop` is measured with rigview `measureArches()`.
- **Lights per car**: J is "aux" (extra lamps) everywhere in code; only `look.lightBar` keeps its name. A light bar beam exists only where the car has a bar (`look.lightBar`); other extra lamps come from `look.lamps.aux` and `look.searchlights`.
- **G-Class** (front double wishbones, rear beam) and **Lancia** (struts all round): springs at the wheel track give much more roll stiffness than inboard beam springs, so the independent axles carry little or no bar (the G-Class's rear beam bar is stiffer instead). Lancia: lower roll centres rolled the outer wheels onto the bump rubbers in every trail corner; more travel or stiffer springs brought back floor strikes and a rollover at 85 km/h; with the Defender's `stallK` its 2.0 stalled in D. Its inner rear wheel lifting in a hard slalom is intended (rally Delta).
- **Static ride**: the Defender's and G-Class's front springs sit at ~40 % of travel; the Lancia and BTR at about half. Stiffer springs to reach 40 % were tried and rejected on the Lancia (its low floor hits crests at 70+; `raise` is clearance, not travel) and the BTR (less droop, worse wheel loads on diagonal blocks). `node tools/suspcheck.mjs` prints the numbers.
- The BTR-82A the player asked for has no downloadable model; that's why the game has the BTR-80.
- **Rejected model, don't download again**: Toyota Land Cruiser J200 (David_Holiday): SketchUp-style export, no textures, most surfaces doubled with a dark see-through copy, tyres in hundreds of pieces.

## BTR-80

- **Model** (`public/models/btr80.glb`; prep config and commands in `assets-src/btr80/`): `tools/prepcar.mjs` handles N wheels (corners `<axle><L|R>`), holder node at each measured hub, tyre material split out (`tire_<k>`), suspension parts split out of the hull by bounding boxes. The physics hub path follows design curves, so the arms stretch a few cm over the travel. `tools/rigview.html?car=btr80` poses the rig by hand (`pose`, `isolate`, `hide`, `turret`, `rays`).
- Steers its first two axles about one centre (`steer.centreZ`, Ackermann); centre-diff groups 1+3 / 2+4 like the real transfer case.
- **Turret and guns** (`turret.js`, `weapons.js`): rounds are point masses with quadratic drag (`k` from the published retained velocity), ray cast per step, ricochet at glancing angles, recoil impulse at the trunnion. Every round is a tracer (a game choice).
- **Muzzle flash**: real photographed flames (`public/fx/muzzle-*.png`, from `assets-src/muzzleflash/`); the strip along the bore must be `DoubleSide`. **Flash light**: a spot and a point glow that stay in the scene at intensity 0 (no recompile), dim by day (`FLASH_DAY`), no shadows (texture units, see Traps).

## Multiplayer (`multiplayer.js`)

- **Transport**: Trystero (WebRTC; public Nostr relays only for introduction, `appId` `offroad-defender-arzgev`), lazy-loaded on first join. Rooms = `?room=<id>`. No TURN server: peers behind symmetric NAT can't connect. The fix is a relay (Cloudflare Durable Object, or Trystero's `ws-relay`); Vercel can't host one.
- **Model**: every player simulates only their own truck and sends a flat array (`S` layout) `SEND_HZ` times a second. The receiver keeps `BUF` snapshots, estimates clock offset as the smallest `now − senderTime` seen (creeping up slowly for drift), plays back `DELAY` behind, extrapolates up to `EXTRAP`.
- **Friends' trucks** use the normal `VehicleView` through a proxy (`makeProxy`), without spot lights (a light per truck changes the lights hash) and without sound.
- **Solid trucks** (setting `solidTrucks`; off = ghosts): the friend's collision boxes on a *dynamic* puppet body with their mass, gravity off, excluded from the heightfield, pulled to the network pose by a critically damped spring (snaps on a large error). A kinematic body was tried: hitting a parked friend stopped us dead and they felt nothing.
- **Test**: two headless Chromes (own ports and `--user-data-dir`) on the same `?room=` URL, `localStorage` preloaded with `autoPause: false` and a name; `game.mp` exposes `peers`, `note`, `follow`.

## Truck model, cockpit and lamps

- **Files**: `src/vehicle/model/index.js` builds a car's model from its look; the Defender's procedural body is in `model/defender/` (`dims.js` measured off `reference.webp`: 2.794 m wheelbase = 459 px in the side view).
- **Greenhouse tumblehome**: everything above the waist is built straight, then `warpGroup(gh, tumblehome(WAIST, k))` scales x by height. Build greenhouse parts at their straight positions. Extrude helpers keep outlines exact (`bevelOffset = -bevel`): draw profiles at final size.
- **Materials** (`materials.js` patches stock materials, `customProgramCacheKey` per option set): `dirt` needs merged geometry in body space (`mergeStatic`); glass and chrome get extra env reflection (`uEnvSpec`), paint stays below 1 (the sky washes red out at grazing angles); glass back faces reflect almost nothing (the env horizon showed as a line inside the cabin); `grain` is a bump height in metres (~0.1–1 mm), and its normalize must be guarded (a NaN pixel is spread over the screen by bloom).
- **Cockpit eye lines** (from the Defender's cockpit eye): windscreen −24° … +10°; steering-rim top at the screen base, 21–25° below the eye line; dials 26–34°, between rim and wheel boss (35°). The binnacle sits on the fascia's front edge (inside the dash shelf its top cut the dials). Keep the housing face behind the dial discs (z-fighting). Recheck these eye lines if you move anything.
- **Lamps** (`lamps.js`): ground irradiance from a lamp at height h is `I·h/r³`, so a plain cone blows out the near ground; beams are spot lights with a half-float `SpotLight.map` cookie encoding I(elevation, azimuth), soft-edged. The cookie projects through the spot's shadow camera; outside it the light is *not* masked, so keep `shadow.focus = 1` and far = distance.
  - One head spot (ahead of the bumper, so no truck geometry is in its frustum) is the only shadow caster, updated only while the head lamps are on. No point lights: every light is evaluated per pixel on all terrain even at zero intensity.
  - Lamp output is scaled by the scene's ambient level so daytime headlights paint no pool.
  - Vertical surfaces 10–20 m ahead get ~100× the ground irradiance: `lamps.js` patches the spot loop of `lights_fragment_begin` with a soft shoulder `E/√(1+(E/K)²)`, `K = LAMP_KNEE`. It coexists with the world's `if (directLight.visible)` wrap of the same loop. Retune K if night exposure changes a lot.
  - **Lamp pairs**: a beam is one spot for two lamps, but wet ground and paint mirror the source, so one spot put the glint between the lamps. `lamps.js` (`lampPairs`, `writeLampPairs`) finds each beam's two lamps from its lens meshes (split at x = 0, only at the spot's end of the car) and the spot loop lights each pixel half from each (`tkLampPair`, world space, the spot recognised by its position); cone, cookie, falloff and shadow stay the spot's. Thin foliage keeps the plain spot. The rear spot is aimed nearly level and wide: aimed at the ground, its own hot spot made the glint ~3 m behind the car.
- **Testing beams**: hide the terrain and props, add a flat 400 m plane with posts at the truck's pose, shoot top-down and cockpit views.

## Vehicle tuning

- **Files**: `vehicle/tuning.js` (schema, `STOCK` built from the car's file, `applySetup`, `analyze`, `RANGES`), `tuningPanel.js`, `colliderView.js` (Show physics; Show suspension draws only its suspension parts), `dyno.js` + `dyno.worker.js`. A new stock setup goes into the car's file (`src/cars/<id>.js`).
- `applySetup(P, setup)` writes into the existing params, so Vehicle, Drivetrain and the view see changes on the next step:
  - *Live*: engine, gears, dampers, bars, travel, brakes, steering, grip, pressures (per axle: `vehicle.pressures`; `[ ]` keep the split).
  - *Eased*: tyre radius and lift (`morphGeometry`); spring rates and mass blend over ~0.5 s. Step changes launched the body at 1 g.
  - *Rebuilt in place*: mass, COM, inertia (`setAdditionalMassProperties`); chassis boxes and wheel cylinders recreated on the same body.
  - Physics geometry lives on the vehicle (`vehicle.R`, `axle.droopY`); the view and overlay read those, not `P`.
- **Lift** lowers `droopY`, so body, colliders and COM rise together and arch room grows. Rubbing is drawn (red tyre outline), not simulated.
- **Defender colliders** are fitted to the visible body (comments in `src/cars/defender.js`); an invisible low nose box was why it couldn't climb steep ramps. Left out on purpose: D-ring shackles and the exhaust (they bend). The spare-wheel box doesn't scale with tyre size.
- **Measure 0–100**: a worker builds its own Rapier world (flat dirt strip) and a `Vehicle` from a copy of the live params; no steering (a lane-keeping correction scrubbed the tyres and doubled the time).
- Closed panel and both overlay switches off cost nothing per frame; Show suspension alone skips the contact queries.

## World and rendering

- **Files**: `world/` (terrain, terrainView, textures, trees, grass, water, props, `colliderStream.js`, `sky.js` + `atmosphere.js`, `environment.js`, `life.js`), `render/` (`pipeline.js` HDR post + SSAO + light shafts, `quality.js`, `cascadeShadow.js`, `shaderPatches.js`); splashes and dust in `effects.js`.
- **The look** (since 2026-10-11, the player's brief): a summer remembered, vivid and alive. Keep the full tonal range: the player rejected lifted shadows, a wide glow and a milky haze as "a cheap filter", but missed the soft dreamy feel once it was gone. So: deep shade, a strong warm sun, an ACES curve and saturated colour, softened by a diffusion glow (`mist`: a blur of the frame that only lightens, so light spills from bright areas into dark ones beside them and what is dark all round stays black) and a hint of lavender in the deepest shade (`shadowCol`, display space, a few hundredths at most). A cool fill from above (`env.hemi`, colours carry the strength, intensity 1; `hemi.userData.level` is the old ambient measure the lamps scale against) keeps shade blue rather than olive, stronger at low sun where the sky light goes yellow. Highlights warm a little (`highCol`), a vignette that leaves the middle clean and closes in towards the corners (towards `vigCol`, never black), a Mie glow round the sun in the haze (`glow`), light shafts (`shafts`). All are time-of-day keys in `environment.js`; the tone curve is `pipeline.params.tone`. The Brightness setting is a gamma on the final display-space picture (`params.gamma`), after the grade and vignette, so it never touches the grade's own balance. Its 100% is a gamma of 0.75 (the player found the grade a little light at 1); the time-of-day keys are tuned under it. The atmosphere has ozone and clear air (`ATM.betaO`, `betaM`): without them a low sun turned everything mustard.
- **Clouds** (`world/clouds.js`, `CLOUDS` levels in `quality.js`): volumetric cumulus in a slab (1.2-3 km; fair-weather tops reach ~40 % of it, a fuller sky grows taller towers), raymarched at 1/4-1/2 resolution with a jittered start and a temporal blend (history reprojected by ray direction), laid onto the sky of the HDR scene by a far-plane quad depth-tested LEQUAL (the dome writes no depth), so bloom and shafts see them. Where clouds are is a procedural 2D coverage (`CLOUD_GLSL`), which every lit material also evaluates in `getSunShadow`: cloud shadows on the ground match the clouds (shared plain-object uniforms `CLOUD`, like `ATMO`). The 3D noise is a 64^3 Perlin-Worley / Worley texture rendered on the GPU at load. The 2D coverage is a baked tileable fbm texture (`CLOUD.noise`, a DataTexture: the clone every material gets shares its source, so one GPU texture); as 16 hashes per call it cost ~0.5 ms of cloud shadows and most of the march. The terrain is at the 16-texture limit at night (lamp cookies), so with `CLOUD_TIGHT` it drops the cloud lookup in that variant. Crisp cumulus needs a high extinction (thin, low-density clouds read as smoke) and a jittered light march (fixed steps banded the self-shadow). Without them (Mobile) the dome's flat cloud layer stays. Painted cumulus towers on the horizon were tried and dropped: flat cut-outs, the player disliked them. Scale cues matter more than detail: big clouds (2-3 km) with soft, uniformly lit bodies read as pasted onto the sky; clouds of a few hundred metres with flat bellies at one height (the plane of bases recedes to the horizon), grey shaded undersides (sky light dims below the cloud's own top) and lit domes read as far and big. Lit clouds near white clip flat in the tone curve: keep their sun gain (`uSunGain`) where the shade sides stay grey. The march covers only the band the tops can reach (steps much longer than a cloud left a fixed grain the temporal blend can't average), the 3D noise is mipmapped and read at a level from the distance, and the composite reads the cloud texture with a small tent filter.
- **Weather** (`world/weather.js`, setting `weather`: fair / changing / rain): one number, the storm `s` (0 fair .. 1 rain under a full deck), drives the look on top of the hour's keys (`environment.weatherLook`: cloud cover, a dimmer sun, haze, a grey sky that also greys the light probe, exposure); rain falls above s ~0.75, the ground gets wet and dries (`uWet` on the terrain: darker, puddles on flat dirt and mud), rings open on the water, wet ground raises no dust, and a clearing shower with the sun under ~40 degrees leaves a rainbow (`pipeline.params.bow`, drawn in the composite from the per-wavelength rainbow angles on the sky and anything beyond ~150 m). Under a storm the fog is greyed and the haze thickened a lot. `weather.set({ s, wet, bow })` freezes a state for tests and screenshots.
- **Ground** (`textures.js`, `terrainView.js`): eight layers of CC0 photo scans (Poly Haven, `public/ground/`, ~4.8 MB, the cliff only drawn on Ultra; `tools/ground-textures.py` rebuilds them), baked at load into texture arrays (the procedural recipes stand in if the AVIFs fail), plus two baked procedural rock structures. AVIF with alpha must be decoded with `createImageBitmap(..., { premultiplyAlpha: 'none' })`: the alpha is height. Roughness and cavity are made in the bake. Tiling is hidden by per-region tile offsets. The meadow, track (and soil) and gravel photos are stretched past their real size (`terrainView.setGround`, settings `groundGrass` / `groundRoad` / `groundGravel`, 2x by default, sliders under Graphics > Ground textures while the player picks the look): at their own size their stones went below a pixel a few metres away and the ground read as one colour; the 1k photos stay sharp enough close up, softening only with the camera right at the ground. The edges between layers are height-blended over `groundSoft` and wander by a few metres of noise (`groundWander`): the land data is 1 m texels, and a narrow height blend drew its edges in straight lines along them. Photo detail alone reads as one flat colour beyond a few metres and adds nothing in motion (the player's verdict on the first photo pass): the look comes from what varies over 5-100 m and from real 3D detail:
  - **Land data** (`bakeLand`, 1 m, `tLand`): lushness from flow accumulation (multiple flow directions on a 2 m grid), hollows and ridges, the side facing the midday sun, height and water nearby, which tints the ground layers and the grass blades (taller and deeper green down the drainage lines, short and golden on dry ridges); scree, rock debris carried down the same flow from steep or rock cells; litter under the trees (`trees.js`: needles, leaves, petals); rock kind by region (limestone, granite, sandstone in the quarry, slate in the pine forest and the stream's gorge). The normal texture's b is sky visibility (horizon search, 8 directions to ~35 m), the terrain's AO. The vista's normals are computed per vertex from its heights: that freed the texture unit `tLand` uses (16 units at night); per pixel they cost four float fetches over most of the sky line.
  - **Ultra** keeps what the other levels trade for speed: ground shading 3 (`GROUND_ULTRA`: the 30 m aerial cliff photo on the rock at half strength, its shapes and colour blotches (at full it took over the rock's own colour and beds); parallax with 10 steps to 13 m, on the meadow too), debris `LEVELS.ultra` (denser scree, every stone and twig shadowing every cascade, no early fade for small pieces) and the full view distances. ~+2.3-5 ms on Ultra against the ground before this pass; High ~+0.6-1.3 ms.
  - **Shader cost**: the terrain shader is register- and branch-sensitive (one compiled program for many paths): ternary chains picking vec3s by kind or layer cost ~0.6 ms at 4 Mpx (now branch-free matrix products: `kindW`), a tint per layer is worked out only for the layers drawn, layer averages are uniforms read back at the bake (`layers.avg`) rather than last-mip fetches. Measure a change by compiling it out (an `if (false)`), not by a uniform toggle.
  - **Rock**: beds from the world height (warped, dipping, bed height and ledge relief by kind), each bed its own tone; ledges where a bed steps back (lit, mossy) and overhangs where the next juts out. Faces and tops are triplanar over two baked structure layers whose rows follow the beds: blocks (tilted, bevelled, joints, streaks; normals baked from the height at float precision, 8 bits banded the lean) and slabs (wavy joints, some closed). Lichen colonies by kind and sun side, moss where it stays damp: bright green patches (the player preferred them to darker cushions). Computing blocks and slabs in the shader cost 2-4 ms; the bake keeps the rock at ~0.6-1.3 ms on High. Even unused, the rock code slowed every terrain pixel (~40 %), so patches with no rock within reach (flags per 16 m cell) draw with a `NO_ROCK` build of the material.
  - **Debris** (`debris.js`): stones (verges and the strip between the ruts, big ones too; in the ruts only grit, pressed in: each stone checks the trail distance where it lands, not at its 1 m cell's centre; scree, gravel; the rock's colour with a little soil, the rock-face photo triplanar at 0.8 m for grain and a bump, soil at the foot; sunk a fifth to a half), cones and twigs under conifers, leaves under broadleaves, petals under blossom (drifting a few metres beyond the crowns, onto the tracks), as four instanced meshes per 16 m chunk generated near the camera; small pieces fade out sooner. Shared vertices with flat shading from derivatives (split vertices were 5x the vertex work). Only the stones over 12 cm cast shadows, into the nearest cascade only (a twin on `NEAR_SHADOW_LAYER`): shadows were half the cost. Stones get less sky light than an open surface (they sit among the ground's relief), else they glowed blue in shade; lit by the warm ground and the blue sky, grey stones still read violet, so their colour loses a little blue (more and they read green). ~0.1-0.8 ms (scree slopes).
  - **Painted stones** (`terrainView.js` stoneCell): beyond the 3D stones (from where they shrink away, `uStoneR` from debris.js configure) the ground paints the same stones: two size classes on 0.4 m and 1.25 m grids, at most one stone a cell, the odds from the ground data at the cell's centre (`stoneDens`, mirrored from debris.js `stoneDensity`: keep the two in step), a flat-topped normal so the real light shades it like the 3D stones' tops (a rounder dome showed its unlit far half: darker than the near stones), a narrow contact shade and the sun's shadow (through `gPomSh`) only while the stone is a few pixels across (smaller, the stone fades into its edge's softening while a hard shadow stayed: dark pits). Drawn while a pixel or two across (measured along the less squeezed screen direction: far ground is squeezed one way only), then their average colour. No new samplers (the terrain is at the 16-unit limit). ~0.1-0.2 ms on Ultra 1440p.
  - Near the camera (< 10 m) parallax occlusion on the dominant layer (its height alpha, 5 cm deep, 6 steps then 3 halvings; Ultra 10 + 4, to 13 m) with the relief's own sun shadow, ~0.4 ms. The relief sinks along the ground's normal, not straight down (straight down, scree seen from below smeared every stone into a streak). The crossing is found by halving the last step: interpolating between the two samples cut the stones' sides into terraces, one per step (the player's "striped stones"). The height blends the tile's two region copies as the colour does, and the relief fades out where the dominant layer changes and where the view runs along the ground: each was a seam or a smear. Crevices darken the sun (microshadows from the cavity). The sun is a `SunLight` (`sunLights[]`, `NUM_SUN_LIGHTS`): code written against `directionalLights` / `NUM_DIR_LIGHTS` silently never runs (the terrain's microshadows didn't for a while).
  - Slopes and faces get warm light bounced off the sunlit ground (the sky probe has no ground): without it rock in shade was lit by the blue sky alone and read navy.
  - Plants on rock: only the undergrowth's rock grass (`uRock`): small dark tufts in clumps, where the moss grows (rockAt's moss terms without the per-pixel ones), not on near-vertical faces, to 45 m. The meadow tufts and ferns on rock read as stickers. Stones out to 48 m on Ultra cost ~2 ms at the lookout for stones too small to see: not worth it.
  - Trails: the trail distance is in the normal texture's alpha: compacted ruts with a dull sheen and tyre tread close up (along the trail: across the trail distance's gradient), loose soil on the verges, short grass between the ruts, rain puddles; dried mud cracks into plates (the slab structure, small) round the mud holes and in the trails' low spots.
  - Wet: after rain the ground dries unevenly (rock, slopes and ridges first; hollows, ruts, mud and the shade last; puddles shrink). Dew glints on the grass at dawn and after a shower (`uDew`, weather.js): sunlit drops on the blades, each turned its own way.
- **Flower fields** (`undergrowth.js` `FIELDS`): lavender and poppy plants reach 80 m at most (at the bush distance of the presets, ~150 m, the two kinds drew 240k plants, +0.7 ms on High); beyond that the grass tips take their colour from the same drift noise, so the far drifts stay.
- **Backlit leaves** (grass, undergrowth, tree crowns and impostors): looking towards the sun, the direct diffuse light is added again, yellowed, by how far the view faces the sun (`gBack` / `lBack`). The direct diffuse already carries the shadow, so only sunlit blades glow; with the old view-independent term a backlit meadow went dull olive.
- **Far land** (`ATMO.atmoVista`, key `vista`): everything beyond the map's edge fades into its own haze, by the length of the view ray outside the map square (so it grows from nothing at the edge and does not thin with height: with the height fog alone far peaks and snowcaps stood out as cut-outs, white under a storm). The general haze (`haze`) was raised a little for the far parts of the map.
- **Volumetric fog** (`render/volumetricFog.js`, key `vfog` times the setting `fog`: off / light / normal / thick, `FOG_AMOUNT` in `environment.js`, normal 0.8; `FOG` levels in `quality.js` following the clouds' level): a low haze (falls off over ~45 m) raymarched at 1/4 resolution (20 steps on High, packed towards the camera, jittered, blended over frames by reprojecting the ray's end; a changed transmittance takes more of the new frame), lit by the sun through the sun's world cascades and the cloud shadows, plus the horizon colour as ambient. The composite upsamples it by depth and applies `scene * T + S` before exposure. It covers 320 m; the far aerial perspective stays analytic in the materials. Its light is the sun's colour washed halfway to white: in the plain sun colour dawn was an orange wall. ~0.6 ms on High.
- **Depth of field** (`render/pipeline.js`, setting `dof`: off / soft / strong, `DOF_AMOUNT` in `main.js`; `DOF` taps per level in `quality.js`, none on Low and Mobile; off in the gunner's sight): the far field only. The blur radius grows from nothing at 60 m towards 0.6 % of the picture's height (soft) far away, so the vista melts and the playable map stays mostly crisp; nothing near the truck blurs (a near field would blur the cockpit). Half resolution: a prefilter takes the scene with its AO and volumetric fog (as the composite sees it), averaging each 2x2 block weighted by blur so a sharp texel doesn't leak into the land behind it; a scatter-as-gather on a Vogel spiral (a texel counts where its own blur reaches); the composite blends it in by the full-res blur, upsampled with weights by blur similarity (no halo at sharp edges). Rain and the life sprites write no depth, so they lower the HDR target's alpha instead (their alpha blend factors) and the depth of field leaves those pixels sharp: without it the rain streaks over the far land were smeared away.
- **Valley mist** (`ATMO.atmoMist`, key `vmist`): a second, shallow exponential fog layer (floor 6 m under the lake, falling off over ~9 m) at dawn, dusk and night: it fills the lake basin and the low meadows and leaves the lookout above it. Its share of the fog is washed towards white in `atmoFogColor`: in the horizon's colour it read as orange smog at sunrise.
- **Ambience** (`audio/ambience.js`, clips in `public/sfx/`, credits in README): birds by day (more at dawn, none in the rain, a burst of song when it clears), a nightingale and owls at night, a two-recording cricket bed (one cricket loop alone sounded metronomic) and a gusting breeze. Each call gets a random side, distance (level and low-pass) and pitch. Clips were picked by having the video model describe them (it hears audio in a black video): several Freesound "nightingale" or "owl" uploads had voices, traffic or the wrong bird. Loops are cross-faded into themselves with ffmpeg and skip the mp3 padding with `loopStart` / `loopEnd`.
- **Life** (`world/life.js`): pollen and dandelion tufts by day, birds in small wheeling flocks by day, petals falling from the 8 nearest trees in blossom (picked on the CPU twice a second), leaves falling in the forest, butterflies over meadows by day (they fly 1.3-2.6 m up: lower, the tall grass hid them; the habitat is judged at the loop's centre, else they popped out over trail edges), fireflies at dusk and night. All on the GPU: a seed per particle, positions from the time, wrapped round the camera; habitats from the ground-data map.
- **Meadow trees**: oaks (`buildOak`) and trees in blossom (`buildBroadleaf` in `foliage.js`, atlas row 3) stand alone or in little groups on open grass. The oak's crown is clumps of cards, each clump's normals pointing out of the clump, with the crown's own shade in a vertex colour (dark inside and under each clump), which the impostor bake multiplies in: one ball of cards lit evenly read as a flat lime sticker near and far. Their cards face out of the crown (`rollFacing`): cards lying along the branches showed edge-on as spikes on the outline. Soft crowns (`opts.soft`) wrap the sunlight round to the shade side and glow through: plain Lambert left one side dull. Impostors take each kind's width (`impostorWidth`): the old fixed half-height width cut round crowns into boxes. Impostors look their sun shadow up a crown's radius towards the light: the shadow pass draws the card turned to the sun, which shaded the far half of the card the viewer sees (a dark underside, "pasted in"); the soft kinds also get the near crowns' wrap light and a rounded normal.
- **Map**: 1024 m, 0.5 m grid (`N = 2048`). Inside r < 140 m the original 400 m map (trail loop, proving ground, big hill), blending into the outer design by 200 m; an 8 km render-only vista (`terrain.far`); invisible walls at the edge.
- **Physics heights on the GPU**: level-0 terrain, grass, undergrowth and water read them from an R32F texture (layout `ix * NN + iz`, so `texelFetch(t, ivec2(iz, ix))`) with Rapier's cell split; trees, rocks and the hut sit on `surfaceHeight`.
- **Trail ride**: `smoothTrailBeds()` low-passes the bed under the trail band (normalised over the driven bed only, so cut walls don't leak in), then adds rut and crown detail back; outer trails get a cut-only grade limit (`maxGrade`). Check with `trailprofile.mjs`, `junction.mjs`, `traillap.mjs` after terrain changes.
- **Light units**: the sun at noon is ~4 scene units, the moon ~0.2 (`sunE` / `moonE` in the `environment.js` keys). Night exposure adapts in a fixed range; a beam-lit road should be a bright mid tone, moonlit ground dim and blue. The lamp peaks and `LAMP_KNEE` were checked against this.
- **Shader traps**:
  - A geometry without a `normal` attribute compiles as `FLAT_SHADED` (no `vNormal`): the grass carries a dummy one.
  - `DoubleSide` flips the normal on back faces: foliage and grass shaded as if facing inward (half the grass black). Foliage uses `NO_FLIP_NORMAL`.
  - Program variants include the output colour space: `compileAsync` must run with the HDR target bound (`compileScene()` in `main.js`).
  - The terrain has no vertex normal, so a zero `vSunShadowWorldNormal` must not be normalised (NaN made every ground lookup read as lit).
- **Trees**: a near crown casts its shadow from a lighter twin (`shadowCrown` in `trees.js`: every other card, scaled up) on `SHADOW_LAYER`, which only the shadow maps draw; the crown itself doesn't cast. Trunk, crown and twin share one instance buffer. On WebGL `alphaTest` on casters is fine: a `customDepthMaterial` (or three's per-material depth clone) keeps it constant, so nothing is re-keyed.
- **Grass** is vertex bound on the M1 Pro, and tiny instances waste the vertex lanes: one 3-vertex blade per instance cost 4x what 8 blades per instance cost (`BATCH`). Every blade sent runs the vertex stage, culled or not. One grid that thins with distance (header of `grass.js`); density, height and width are curves over distance (`evalCurve`, per level in `quality.js` `grassCurve`), tuned by eye by the player. The sun is a `SunLight` (`sunLights[0]`), not in `directionalLights`. `grass.setTiming(renderer, true)` times it on the GPU.
- **Quality presets** (`quality.js`, applied live): every field has a `g`-prefixed setting (`presetToGfx` / `gfxToQuality`); changing one switches `quality` to `custom`. Grass and bushes is the `gVeg` row (off / low / medium / high / ultra, `vegOf`): picking a level makes quality Custom. Rough GPU cost is in the header of `quality.js`.
- **Auto quality** (`updateAutoQuality` in `graphics.js`, called from the rAF loop only, so `game.tick` tests don't trigger it): ladder low → ultra; start = the preset saved for this GPU + screen (`offroad.autoQuality.v1`, which then only steps down) or a guess from the GPU name. < 45 fps → one down; ≥ 57 → try one up, keep it only if it holds 50, else the ceiling stays below. A hidden pane doesn't measure; a throttled but visible one measures low.
- **Mobile preset** (Auto on phones and tablets, `isMobileDevice` incl. iPadOS): no AA, shadows, SSAO or grass; `updateDynamicResolution` (`graphics.js`) moves the pixel density in steps to hold 45–60 fps and only resizes buffers, never recompiles.
- **Sun shadow** (`render/cascadeShadow.js`, `SHADOWS` in `quality.js`): one depth atlas holding the world's 1–2 soft cascades (a smooth 3×3 filter of hardware compares, `soft` metres wide) and then the player's car in its own small sharp tile (`setCar`: the car's meshes go on `CAR_LAYER` and into no world cascade; the tile is texel-snapped and sized by the car's bounding sphere, and the shader multiplies it in only where the point projects along the light onto that sphere). three's `WebGLShadowMap` tests object layers against the *view* camera, not the shadow camera, so `attach()` sets the view camera's layers per tile while the shadow maps render: world tiles 0 + `SHADOW_LAYER` (shadow-only proxies), the car's tile `CAR_LAYER`, the lamps 0 + `SHADOW_LAYER`. Friends' cars stay on layer 0 (soft shadows). The atlas is made in `cascadeShadow.js` (`_makeMap`) like three makes it but with a one-channel colour target: nothing reads the colour, and RGBA cost bandwidth every frame.
- **Car batches** (`vehicle/model/batched.js`): the car's parts that share a material are one `BatchedMesh` each; the meshes stay as stand-ins on no layer, and `VehicleView.update` ends with `batch.sync()`, which copies their transforms and visibility. Move, hide or scale the stand-ins, never the batches. Not batched: transparent materials and meshes with their own depth material (the deforming tyres); a new per-mesh shader uniform needs the same.
- **SSAO** (`pipeline.js`): flat ground must read exactly 1.0 (check `pipeline.ao[1]` via `readRenderTargetPixels` into a Uint16Array of half floats). That needs depth samples snapped to full-res texel centres, central-difference normals except across depth edges, a distance-scaled occluder threshold, and a 4×4 Bayer rotation with a depth-aware 4×4 blur averaging exactly one period. Without these flat ground showed resolution-dependent bands / dot grids.
- **Dust**: point size scales with `projectionMatrix[1][1]` and the render target height, so puffs look the same at any resolution, DPR and fov. Capped at `PUFF_CAP`; wheelspin adds little, so a car stuck spinning makes a haze, not a wall.
- **Load**: ~3 s to "Ready"; `game.loadLog` has stage times.

## Time of day (`world/environment.js`)

- **One number**: the hour, 0..24. `env.setHour(h, { instant })` is the only entry point (`APPLY.time` in `main.js`): `instant` for the slider, buttons and startup, otherwise a sweep *forward* over `ANIM_TIME` (N key).
- **Key frames** (`KEYS`) with a periodic monotone cubic between them (no overshoot, so a key renders exactly its own values). Day / Dusk / Night are keys at `QUICK_HOURS`. To add a look, add a key.
- **Sun and moon follow arcs** (`SUN_ARC`, `MOON_ARC`); a key's `pin: [elev, azim]` puts the sun exactly where that preset had it.
- **Continuous, never a mode switch**: direct light = sun + moon, each fading with its own height; fog and sky colours = a sun part (kept until 14° below: twilight) + a moon part. A sun below the horizon dip keeps the horizon's last dim red transmittance, else it would jump to 0.
- **Exposure** `auto` 0..1 cross-fades fixed exposure and eye adaptation in log space; the luminance pass runs only while `auto > 0.001`. Blue-hour and pre-dawn keys run fully adaptive, else the frame dips to black between sun and moon.
- **`env.darkness`** (0..1) drives everything that fades; **`env.night`** is the same with hysteresis, only for decisions that change the lights hash (lamp visibility, automatic headlights via `env.onNightChange`).
- **Light probe**: three's `fromScene()` does the whole PMREM prefilter in one frame (a 35 ms hitch); `updateProbe` spreads it over ~15 frames using PMREMGenerator internals (three is pinned) and falls back to `fromScene` if they're missing. `env.settle()` finishes it synchronously (tests, screenshots).
- **Testing**: `game.env.setHour(h, { instant: true })`, `game.env.settle()`, then tick; `game.setPaused(true)` and `game.redraw = 1e9` for a static scene.

## Sound

- **Engine start** (`STARTER_DEFAULTS` in `engine-worklet.js`, `audio.setStarterTune()` live): tuned by ear by the player, don't retune unasked. Rejected: broadband noise only ("a dog sniffing"), sine tones with crank wobble ("a toy ray gun"); narrow-band noise at the gear mesh works.
- Engine timbre has little energy at 200–600 Hz (a real V8 has harmonic bands there), so low-load rpm changes are heard mostly as loudness. Keep low-rpm lugging quieter than high-rpm WOT. Fixed-length noise clicks per exhaust pulse sounded like a ticking motorboat: pulses last a fixed crank angle.
- **Impact sounds: removed on purpose, don't add back unasked.** Rejected by ear: the old synthesised knock (a toy sound, fired on every landing), Freesound "S021 Metal Impact" and nine synthesised variants. If picked up again: real recordings picked by the player, trigger only on bump-stop entry, separate tyre-landing and body-contact sounds (body contacts need Rapier `CONTACT_FORCE_EVENTS`), muffled in the cockpit.
