# Dev notes

What the code can't tell you: conventions, measured baselines, traps, rejected options, known limits.

> Written by earlier agents. Treat every claim as a hint: check it against the code or a test, and fix the note when it is wrong.

## Conventions

- **Vehicle frame** (physics and model): `+x` right, `+y` up, **`-z` forward**. `y = 0` is the ground at static ride height on the stock setup (bigger tyres and lift raise the body: `rideRaise`), `z = 0` mid-wheelbase. Defender axles at `z = ∓1.397`.
- **Wheel spin** `ω > 0` = rolling forward = rotation about `-x`. Tyre torque on the wheel is `-Fx * Re`.
- **Axle roll** `phi > 0` = right side up = rotation about body `+z`. Axle compression `c` is measured at the springs (`springTrack`), not at the wheels.
- **Steer** `+1` = right. Wheel yaw rotation is `-steer` about `y`.
- **Rapier heightfield**: `heights[ix * (N+1) + iz]`, columns = x, rows = z, centred on the collider. Each cell is split along the diagonal **(x1, z0)–(x0, z1)**. `terrainView.js` uses the same split; if they differ, wheels float or sink.
- Rapier scene queries see colliders only after a `world.step()`. Wheel rays exclude the vehicle's own body (this also excludes the wheel side cylinders).

## How the physics fits together

- Rapier at **240 Hz** (`H` in `main.js`); each step `Vehicle.step()` casts the ray fans once, then runs **4 substeps** (960 Hz) and applies the averaged forces. The tyre relaxation and damper terms are stable only because of 960 Hz: raise substeps if you add stiffness.
- **Axle DOF** uses absolute velocities (`vz`, `Om`); body-relative rates are `vz - vMountU`, `Om - rollRateBody`. Unsprung gravity along `up` is cancelled on the Rapier body, because the axle DOF carries it.
- **Force split**: the tyre-force component along body-up goes into the axle DOF, the rest acts on the chassis at the hub (roll centre at axle height, panhard rod). The roll moment from side force at the contact patch goes into the axle roll; removing that is what let the truck corner at 1 g without tipping.
- **Tyre model** (`tire.js`):
  - Deflection `ux`, `uy` are clamped by the steady-state slip so a spinning wheel can't wind up; a low-speed damper (`2.6 * Fn * fade`) stops parked trucks oscillating.
  - Radial damping `P.tire.damping` = 650 N·s/m (rolling, 20 psi), scaled by `kScale`, `(20/psi)^0.6` and `(1 + 2·exp(−v/1.5))` (`tireRadialDamping`): aired down damps more (10 psi ×1.52, 32 psi ×0.75), creeping ~3× more than rolling. The shocks do ~10× more, so the Defender barely changes; BTR at 10 psi over a 6 cm log at 30 km/h no longer leaves the ground (was 175 ms) and the hull's peak jolt halves. `node tools/tirehop.mjs bump|wash [kmh] [psi]` (`CAR=`).
  - Lateral load sensitivity: peak slip angle grows as `Fn^0.35` (`latPeak`), so cornering stiffness ~`Fn^0.65`. Before, it was ~proportional to load: load transfer cost no grip, weight split and bars barely changed the balance.
- **Contact patch** (`Vehicle.castContact` / `integrateTyre`): 39 rays, ground between rays linear, `TIRE_SUB` = 6 samples between rays, force `K·√δ` per angle. The tread band is stiff (loaded profile = upper envelope of the raw one under parabolas of curvature `R / (2·TIRE_BELT)`): without it a step edge cut into the tyre and gave 34 m/s² spikes. `K` from `tireRadialStiffness(psi)` keeps flat-ground stiffness (static squash 3.19 → 3.28 cm). Cost ~0.55–0.75 ms per physics step for 4 wheels (was ~0.5). `node tools/tiretest.mjs rock|step [kmh] [psi]` (`CAR=`); Defender at 20 psi: rock at 5 km/h 10.0 kN, 24 rays loaded (old single point 10.5 kN); 15 cm step 11.5 kN, 1.9 m/s² (old 15.8 kN).
- **Tilted tyres and hard landings** (`tools/droptest.mjs`, `tools/resettest.mjs`): after R on a hillside or a truck kicked onto its side, peak tyre loads were 100–550 kN (static 5.5). Causes, fixed: the rim strike was a near-elastic 2.5 MN/m spring (now gives back only `RIM_GIVE_BACK` = 15 %); the tilted normal `n_wheelplane + axle·s` left a 56° tilt at 40°, so side grip could hold a rolled truck on its tyre shoulders (now `n_wheelplane·√(1−s²) + axle·s`, exact for a plane); the outer ray rows sit at 0.34 × width but the tread runs to 0.5, so the downhill edge sank ~10 cm unfelt (they now add the overhang × s). Worst on-side reset on the map 550 → 110 kN. `resettest.mjs` drops the truck level from 1 m at random spots (`ROLL=90` on its side). Also: traction control's cap (2400 Nm, the Defender's) now scales with `P.brakes.front / 2700`; before, eight spinning BTR wheels kept it on a 30° ramp at full throttle for good.
- **Physics ground = drawn ground** (`node tools/groundmatch.mjs`): terrain rays vs `surfaceHeight` match within 0.04 mm over 20 000 points. A boulder's collider is the convex hull of its vertices and the drawn rock is pushed out onto that hull (`props.js addRock`, ~170 ms at load). Hull above mesh: median 0.1 mm, 90 % 8 mm, 99 % 29 mm (before 3 / 48 / 171 mm: the tyre floated over dents).
- Open diffs need no special code in `drivetrain.js`: the gear row with `-G/4` per wheel already splits equally.
- **Torque reactions**: body gets `-(Ie*αe + Tprop_f + Tprop_r)` about `+z`, each axle `+Tprop` (launch body roll, axle wrap); sign follows a crank turning clockwise seen from the front.
- **Handling balance** (`tools/yawtest.mjs`): ~1.9°/g understeer at the road wheels on dirt; lifting off at the limit tucks the nose in a little and settles. It comes from real parts, not a stability aid: tyre load sensitivity, 51 % front weight, ~53 % front roll stiffness (springs 50000 / 47000, ARB 7500 / 4000), steering compliance (`steer.stiffness`, `Vehicle.steerCompliance`) and rear roll understeer (`rollSteer` 0.06 rad/rad). Yaw inertia includes the axles (4400).
- Roll damping matters most for the suspension: at ζ≈0.2 the truck rocked itself onto two wheels.
- **Traction control** (`vehicle.tc`, key Y) brakes any wheel faster than the ground under it so open diffs send torque to wheels with grip; full authority below ~30 km/h.
- **Auto upshift** uses `min(wheel speed, ground speed)`; otherwise it upshifted while the wheels spun in the rock garden.

## UI (HUD, menu, input)

- **`BINDINGS` in `input.js` is the single source of truth** for controls (dispatch, HUD hints, toast key caps, menu Controls page). To add one: a binding with an `id`, then a handler in `ACTIONS` in `main.js`.
- Discrete actions fire from the key event (`input.onAction`), not the game loop, so they work while driving frames by hand with `game.tick`. On Cmd keydown the held keys are dropped (macOS sends no keyup for them); the F row except F3 and all Cmd/Ctrl/Alt combos go to the browser.
- **HUD cost rules**: no layout reads per frame; DOM text/classes written only on change (cache in `hud.c`); bars move with `transform`; rpm dial redraws only on a ≥ 20 rpm move. Measured: HUD ≈ 0.02 ms/frame, < 1 DOM mutation per frame.
- **Touch** (`touch.js`): pointer capture on every control (multi-finger; sliding off keeps the control); `touch.release()` on pause prevents stuck gas. The device becomes `'touch'` after a tap only *after* the tap finishes (switching re-renders the UI and would eat the click). The fullscreen button belongs to the HUD, shows on every device and pulses red until first used (`offroad.fullscreenUsed.v1`). Touch steering counts as analog (the gamepad speed curve, not the keyboard assist). Test with Playwright `hasTouch` + CDP `Input.dispatchTouchEvent`.
- **Testing the UI**: dispatch `KeyboardEvent`s on `window`; `game.action('camera')` runs an action; a fake gamepad works by overriding `navigator.getGamepads`. The browser pane can't screenshot HTML overlays reliably at emulated sizes larger than the pane: use `tools/cdp.mjs` (headless Chrome over CDP).

## Traps already hit

- **Wheel side cylinders must never touch the terrain heightfield.** They are teleported to the hub every step, so ground contact becomes a huge impulse on the whole truck (10–37 g spikes, the "pogo stick" ride). Heightfields are `GROUP_GROUND` (set in the Vehicle constructor); the cylinders filter it out and only hit rocks, logs and trees.
- **Don't fix understeer with rear roll stiffness.** Bars were once moved from 11000 / 4000 to 8000 / 8500 to cure keyboard understeer; with rear-biased weight the truck went nearly neutral and spun on lift-off at speed. The real cause was the keyboard asking for 4× the angle (next item). Saved setups with exactly 8000 / 8500 migrate to the stock bars (`fill` in `tuning.js`, setup `v` 2).
- **Keyboard steering at speed is limited by lateral acceleration** (`applyInput`, setting `steerAssist`, `STEER_ASSIST` in `Vehicle.js`): a held key asks for the angle that corners at k × the grip limit (`gripG` = 0.80 × mean μ of tyres in contact, smoothed 0.3 s) + c × the front tyres' peak slip angle; strong k 1.2 c 0.5, light k 1.6 c 0.6, off = full lock. The gamepad keeps its own speed curve. Input filter only: flooring the gas at the limit can still spin the truck, by design. `node tools/steerassist.mjs` (`FULL=1` floors the gas). Without it, full lock at 45 km/h asked for 4× the needed angle and rocked the truck onto two wheels.
- With no one steering, the truck wanders slowly under full power (axle wrap tilts the axles ~0.3–0.9°, rear roll steer turns that into ~0.2°/s of yaw). Real, but headless runs need room (the dyno pad is 160 m wide).
- `flat` is a reserved GLSL word (the tyre shader failed to compile).
- three r186 removed `PCFSoftShadowMap`; use `PCFShadowMap`. Use `NeutralToneMapping`: ACES made red paint look salmon.
- The Sky shader is very bright. Bloom must stay off in daytime and `scene.environmentIntensity` low (0.22 day), or everything washes out white.
- Screen-space bump mapping in the terrain shader wrecked the normals and hid all ground shadows. For ground detail use a real normal map.
- Canvas `putImageData` premultiplies alpha and destroys colour where alpha is low. Procedural textures go straight into `DataTexture`s.
- Terrain features narrower than ~2 grid cells (0.5 m) alias into washboard bumps on diagonal trails (happened with ruts). Keep features wide.
- Trail junctions: blend the trails' smoothed heights by weight, and compute the end corrections **before** mutating the array (a bug left a 2 m cliff).
- Headlights default off in daytime: each shadow-casting spotlight is a full extra shadow pass for almost no visible light.

## Testing workflow

- **Headless**: `npm run simtest [settle|accel|brake|slope|climb|turn|manual|cars|btr|layouts|suspension]` checks bands around the Baselines and exits 1 on any FAIL. `VERBOSE=1` prints traces; `TUNE='{json}'` only prints (`info`) the stock bands. A band that a deliberate physics change moves goes in the same commit, with the new number in Baselines. Known, not checked: in manual with the auto-clutch, lifting off on a 30° climb in 1st low opens the clutch and the truck rolls back (~18 km/h after 4 s); the automatic holds.
- Ride/handling tools: `tools/traillap.mjs [kmh] [cornerG] [secs] [analog|digital]` (`digital` mimics keyboard steering), `handling.mjs [surface]`, `bumptest.mjs [kmh] [height] [length] [both|left]`, `climb.mjs [surface] [high|low] [humps] [open,centre,all]` (`NOTC=1` turns TC off), `terraintest.mjs`, `junction.mjs`. `handling`, `yawtest`, `traillap`, `steerassist` take `CAR=`.
- **Browser**: `window.game` (`tick(dt)`, `autopilot`, `placeVehicle`, `action(id)`, `timings`, `loadLog`, ...). `shot()` captures only the WebGL canvas; for HUD and menus use `tools/cdp.mjs`.
- A hidden pane or background tab throttles `requestAnimationFrame`: FPS readings are meaningless and screenshots stale. Drive with `game.tick` and capture with `tools/shotserver.py` plus `shot()` from `tools/browser-snippets.js` (which also has the lane and trail drivers).
- Engine sound: `node tools/enginesound.mjs out.wav` renders the worklet through a fixed script and prints each segment's level. Keep low-rpm lugging quieter than high-rpm WOT. Exhaust pulses last a fixed crank angle; fixed ~1.5 ms noise clicks sounded like a ticking motorboat at idle. Audio-model reviews of the WAV (seed via OpenRouter) were inconsistent: weak evidence.

## Truck model, cockpit and lamps

- **Files**: `src/vehicle/model/index.js` builds a car's model from its look; the Defender's procedural body is in `model/defender/` (`dims.js` measured off `reference.webp`: 2.794 m wheelbase = 459 px in the side view).
- **Greenhouse tumblehome**: everything above the waist is built straight, then `warpGroup(gh, tumblehome(WAIST, k))` scales x by height (normals follow). Build greenhouse parts at their straight positions. Extrude helpers keep outlines exact (`bevelOffset = -bevel`): draw profiles at final size.
- **Materials** (`materials.js` patches stock materials, `customProgramCacheKey` per option set):
  - `dirt` needs merged geometry in body space (`mergeStatic` gives it).
  - `env`: `scene.environmentIntensity` is low for the terrain, so glass/chrome need more; paint stays below 1 because the sky PMREM washes red paint out at grazing angles.
  - `glass`: back faces reflect almost nothing (inside the cabin the env horizon showed as a line across the screen).
  - `grain`: noise bump height in metres (~0.1–1 mm); a unit-height bump at 420 cycles/m looked like asphalt. Guard the normalize: a NaN pixel is spread over the screen by bloom (black cockpit at night).
- **Cockpit geometry**: eye `(-0.42, 1.76, 0.10)`, fov 62, pitch −0.14. Windscreen spans −24° … +10° from the eye; rim top at the screen base (21–25° below the eye line); dials (26–34°) between rim and wheel boss (35°). The binnacle sits on the fascia's front edge, in front of the dash shelf (`DT = 1.40`); inside the shelf, its top cut the lower half of every dial. Keep the housing face behind the dial discs (z-fighting doubled the numbers). Recheck these eye lines if you move anything.
- **Lamps** (`lamps.js`): ground irradiance from a lamp at height h is `I·h/r³`, so a plain cone blows out the near ground; beams are spot lights with a `SpotLight.map` cookie encoding I(elevation, azimuth); edges are soft because hard ones looked harsh. Peaks: low 36 000 cd, high ≈ 65 000 (`highGain` 0.68), aux 55 000, searchlight 100 000 (before 45 000 / 118 000 / 85 000). Cookies are half-float (8-bit banded in the near field). The cookie projects through the spot's shadow camera (fov = 2·angle, far = distance); outside it the light is *not* masked, so keep `shadow.focus = 1` and far = distance.
  - One head spot (in front of the bumper hoop, so no truck geometry is in its frustum) is the only shadow caster, updated only while the head lamps are on. No point lights.
  - Lights are `visible = false` in the day unless switched on; at night they stay visible at zero intensity so switching lamps never recompiles. The day→night switch recompiles once; a `renderer.compileAsync` prewarm with lights visible would remove that hitch (needs a hook in `main.js`). Lamp output is scaled by the scene's ambient level so daytime headlights paint no pool.
  - Vertical surfaces 10–20 m ahead in the hot zone get ~100× the ground irradiance (`I/d²` vs `I·h/r³`): driving at a bank blew out and bloomed the windscreen. `lamps.js` patches `ShaderChunk.lights_fragment_begin` (spot loop only; only the truck has spot lights) with a soft shoulder `E/√(1+(E/K)²)`, `K = LAMP_KNEE = 6` (9 before: near banks sat on the cap, so dimmer lamps didn't dim them). Beam-lit road is ~0.6–4.5, barely touched. It coexists with the world branch's `if (directLight.visible)` wrap of the same loop (both regexes match either order). Retune `K` if night exposure changes a lot.
- **Budget**: 110 meshes after merging (79 shadow casters), ~160 k triangles (tyres ~25 k each), 212 draw calls by day. The old truck's 7 lights were evaluated per pixel on all terrain even at zero intensity; GPU frame time fell ~25–30 % by day and ~10–25 % at night (headless Chrome 1920×1080 @ DPR 1.75, interleaved, GPU shared).
- **Testing beams**: hide `scene.children[0]` / `[1]` (terrain, props), add a flat 400 m plane with posts at the truck's pose, shoot top-down and cockpit views; real terrain makes beam shapes hard to judge.

## Cars (one file per car: `src/cars/<id>.js`, format and add-a-car recipe in `src/cars/README.md`)

- **Every car is a file** (physics in the params' own vocabulary, looks, test bands), listed in `src/cars/index.js`. `vehicle/carParams.js` `makeCarParams(id)` is the one builder for any axle count/type; the Defender is an ordinary car (its hand-set `droopY` is kept and marked). The refactor was verified: stock-applied params equal the old ones, `VERBOSE=1 npm run simtest` identical line for line, model screenshots pixel-identical. Intended differences: raw params carry the car's own engine; the Defender's tyre ranges follow the other cars' rule (29–38" in 0.5" steps); the HUD shows a swapped engine's name for every car.
- **Cut parts, lenses, arches.** `tools/cutparts.mjs` moves connected pieces inside a box into their own node (keeps meshopt and textures): G-Class and Lancia steering wheels (clockwise for a right turn, like the Defender's), G-Class lamp glass, Lancia driving lamps (cut out of the LightBump mesh with `material`). `physics.archTop` measured with rigview `measureArches()`: Defender 1.01 (inner wheel tubs; openings 1.03), G-Class 0.86, Lancia 0.67, BTR 1.22. The tuning panel's front / rear readouts are the first and last axle.
- **Lights per car.** No light bar where the car has none (the G-Class got one only because the Defender has it); the bar beam is built only from `look.lightBar` or `lamps.bar`. J is "aux" (extra lamps) everywhere (`auxLights`, `view.lights.aux`, `model.lights.aux`, `lamps.aux`, lens role `aux`, `BEAM.aux`); only `look.lightBar` keeps its name. Lancia: L = four outer headlamps, J = four grille driving lamps; front indicators glow amber, not red with the brake. BTR-80: head beam at the four round headlamps' height, ahead of the nose (a beam behind the nose plate lit the plate white); J = two searchlights (`look.searchlights`, IR filter covers drawn open), the turret's riding the gun cradle. **A searchlight has no cookie on purpose**: every SpotLight map takes a texture unit in every lit material, and two cookie spots made the BTR scene ask for 17 of 16 units (three.js warning, broken materials on most GPUs).
- **Each car's own controls.** `vehicle/controls.js` lists the controls a car lacks (from drivetrain, lamps, turret); `input.js setCarControls` / `hasControl` drop them from keys, pad, touch, menu, controls page, settings rows (`hot`), touch drawer, tuning switches and 'stuck' callouts. The rpm dial reads to the limiter rounded up to a thousand (BTR 3, Defender 6, G-Class and Lancia 7) and redraws when tuning swaps the engine. Removed because the real cars lack them: the Lancia's low range (tuning keeps it out of old setups), the G-Class's 2WD switch.
- **Drive layouts.** `drive.layout`: 'awd' (default), 'parttime' (no centre diff: 2WD at start, front and rear locked together in 4WD, `Drivetrain.centreLocked`), 'rwd', 'fwd'. `npm run simtest layouts`: torque share 0.50 / 0 / 0 / 1, part-time 4WD keeps prop speeds equal. Independent axles on a two-axle car work (`npm run simtest suspension`).
- **G-Class and Lancia on their real suspensions.** G-Class: double wishbones in front (`rcHeight` 0.10, `camberGain` −0.3, unsprung 150 kg estimate; the 55 kg the beam lost moved into `bodyMass` 2105 so kerb weight stays 2450), five-link beam behind kept. Lancia: struts all round (`rcHeight` 0.12 / 0.16 because its 6 cm gravel lift raises them; 0.06 / 0.10 rolled the outer wheels onto the bump rubbers in every trail corner). Springs at the wheel track give much more roll stiffness than inboard beam springs (G front 72 vs 39 kN·m/rad), so the bars were cut: G front 0, rear 4500 → 20000 (4500 let the inner front wheel leave the ground ~1 s in the 70 km/h slalom); Lancia 0 / 2000 (N/m at the wheels), bump damping 2300 / 1700 → 3000 / 2200. More travel or stiffer springs on the Lancia brought back floor strikes and a rollover at 85 km/h. Result vs beams: circle grip and roll equal or better (G 0.60 g, 6.5°; Lancia 0.75 g, 5.6°), lift-off yaw still settles, trail bump-stop time 0.7–1.8 %, G trail peak jolt at 85 km/h 35 → 48 m/s² (no body contacts), the Lancia's inner rear wheel lifts ~0.7 s in the 60–70 km/h slalom (it didn't on beams). 0–100 and ride height unchanged; Defender and BTR byte-identical. `difftest`: G 40 %, Lancia 46.9 % (was 48.4); right side on ice G 0.8 km/h (was −5.3), Lancia 44 (37). Saved setups: setup `v` 3, and a car's `suspensionV` drops the suspension section of older setups (an independent bar is N/m at the wheels, not N·m/rad: an old G bar of 9000 would be 24 kN·m/rad). `tools/suspcheck.mjs` and the tuning panel count an independent bar as `arb · track²` (the panel had half that).

## BTR-80 and N-axle vehicles (Oct 5)

- **N axles**: wheels are `2a` (left) and `2a+1` (right) of axle `a`, front to back. Static loads: lever rule for 2 axles, a rigid body on equal springs for more (`axleShares`). The BTR steers its first two axles about one centre (`steer.centreZ`, Ackermann). The 2-axle cars gave byte-identical simtest numbers through the change.
- **Independent corners** (`Vehicle.cornerSubstep`): one DOF per wheel; `rcHeight` is the side force's share into the corner (`q = rc / (track/2)`), the rest goes to the body at the patch. BTR: torsion bars 90 kN/m per corner, double wishbones (rc 0.15 m, camber −0.2 rad/m), 0.32 m travel, dampers on axles 1 and 4 twice as strong as 2 and 3 (two shocks per wheel, as on the real one).
- **Drive layout** (`driveLayout(P)`): centre-diff groups (`axles[i].group`; BTR 1+3 / 2+4, the real transfer case), axles in a group rigidly linked; cam-type LSD axle diffs (row bound `lock·|axle torque| + 15 Nm`). Centre split `drive.centreSplit` (group 0's share; epicyclic: the gear, park and hold rows weight wheels `s / n0` and `(1 − s) / n1`); 0.5 = the old equal rows, so Defender and BTR are byte-identical; G 500 0.4, Lancia 0.47. `drive.centre: 'viscous'`: the centre lock is bounded by `viscous` Nm per rpm of prop slip (no driver lock). `node tools/difftest.mjs`: the split on a full-throttle start, and speed after 4 s with the front axle or right side on ice. With TC on (default) the Lancia went 58 → 64 km/h (front on ice) and −8 (spun round) → 37 (right side on ice); with TC off and full throttle in 1st its Torsen and coupling spin the dirt wheels too and it yaws round on split grip (a driver would lift). Steady circle on dirt 0.76 → 0.74 g (Torsen understeer). With several handbrake axles each gets its own row: one row on the mean let the groups counter-rotate through the centre diff and held nothing (the BTR slid off a 30° slope).
- **Tyre nominal load** `P.tire.fnRef` (default 5200 N, the 33" tyre): load sensitivity is relative to it. Without it the BTR's 17 kN per wheel cut μ by 25 % (clamped) and it couldn't stand on 30°.
- **Driveline losses** `P.tire.hubDragV` (BTR 4.5 Nm per rad/s per wheel: hub reductions, eight wheel drives, oil churning; ~54 kW at 80 km/h): without it the BTR ran to its governor at 91 km/h instead of 80. The tuning panel's top speed and 0–60 include it (78 km/h / 27.5 s vs physics 80 / 24.4).
- **Manual-only cars** (`P.manualOnly`): "automatic" is the 5-speed box with `autoShift` (rpm-based, 1 s between shifts). At full throttle it upshifts only if the next gear would still accelerate; without that a 30° climb in 1st low shifted to 2nd at the governor and bogged down.
- **Traction control on a rollback**: rolling backwards with wheels driven forwards counts as wheelspin, so TC brakes them and the vehicle slides on (standing start on 30°; the auto-clutch takes ~0.5 s to bite). The simtest climbs from a run-up, like a real gradient test.
- **Model** (`public/models/btr80.glb`, 3.0 MB; prep config and commands in `assets-src/btr80/`): `tools/prepcar.mjs` handles N wheels (corners `<axle><L|R>`), holder node at each measured hub, tyre material split out (`tire_<k>`), suspension parts split out of the hull by bounding boxes (`parts`). Arms are steep in the model but the physics hub path follows design curves, so they stretch a few cm over the travel. `tools/rigview.html?car=btr80` poses the rig by hand (`pose`, `isolate`, `hide`, `turret`, `rays`) for underside shots. The BTR's own tyres (`BTR_80_Tire`) use the deform shader; rims don't move (`rim` radius in `makeTireMesh`).
- **Turret and guns** (`turret.js`, `weapons.js`): rounds are point masses with quadratic drag (`k` from the published retained velocity), ray cast per step, ricochet at glancing angles, recoil impulse at the trunnion. Every round is a tracer (`tracerEvery` 1, a game choice; real belts load one in four or five).
- **Muzzle flash** (`MuzzleFlash`, textures `public/fx/muzzle-*.png` from `assets-src/muzzleflash/`, real photographed flames): the strip along the bore must be `DoubleSide` (half the time it faces away).
- **Flash light**: a spot (half-angle 80°, 200 cd × `flash`, 45 m) and a point glow (3 cd, 3.5 m), warm 2200 K, in the scene from the start at intensity 0 (no lights-hash change, no recompile); by day they keep 12 % (`FLASH_DAY`). **No shadows**: a shadowed point light adds a `pointShadowMap` sampler to every lit program, and the terrain shader already binds 14 of 16 texture units (it hit 17: "Trying to use 17 texture units"). The cone does the hull's job. Night fire at 600 rpm held 60 fps.

## Multiplayer (Oct 4, `multiplayer.js`)

## World and rendering

- **Files**: `world/` (terrain, terrainView, textures, trees, grass, water, props, `colliderStream.js`, `sky.js` + `atmosphere.js`, `environment.js`), `render/` (`pipeline.js` HDR post + SSAO, `quality.js`, `cascadeShadow.js`, `shaderPatches.js`); splashes in `effects.js`.
- **Map**: 1024 m, 0.5 m grid (`N = 2048`). Inside r < 140 m the original 400 m map is unchanged (trail loop, proving ground, big hill), blending into the outer design by 200 m. Edge mountains continue into an 8 km render-only vista (`terrain.far`); invisible walls at the map edge. Generation ≈ 1.1 s. `node tools/mapimage.mjs out.png` renders the layout.
- **Consistency with the physics**: level-0 terrain patches, grass, undergrowth and water read the physics heights (R32F texture, layout `ix * NN + iz`, so `texelFetch(t, ivec2(iz, ix))`) with Rapier's cell split; trees, rocks and the hut sit on `surfaceHeight`.
- **Trail ride quality** (user complaint: bouncy): `smoothTrailBeds()` low-passes the bed under the trail band (Gaussian σ 2 m, normalised over the driven bed only so cut walls don't leak in), then adds rut/crown detail back; outer trails get a cut-only grade limit (`maxGrade`). Checks: `node tools/trailprofile.mjs 65` (crests that unload the truck; `BASE=2` for sharper features) must list none; `node tools/junction.mjs` (steepest trail-centre slope, 19.2°); `traillap.mjs 60 0.45 90`. Effect: wheel off ground 5.8 → 2.6 %, vertical accel rms 2.65 → 1.93 m/s², max roll 9.2 → 8.0°.
- **Static colliders are streamed** (`ColliderStream`, 32 m chunks, ±2 around the truck; trees have their own copy). Rapier's step cost grows with every collider, static, far or *disabled* (~0.12 µs per collider per 240 Hz step: 800 boulders = 0.4 ms/frame); only removal helps. Physics per frame went from ~1.5–2.3 ms (1100 trunks + 500 rocks always in) to ~0.7–1.0 ms.
- **Light units and the night**: sun ≈ 4 scene units at noon, moon 0.22 (moonlit ground ≈ 0.1–0.15). Night exposure adapts in 1.0–3.2 (key 0.06): a beam-lit road at 1–7 units is a bright mid tone, moonlit ground dim and blue; night bloom threshold 1.4 (after exposure), strength 0.06. The lamp peaks and K = 6 were checked against this; retune K if the exposure range changes. Grass and foliage respond to lamps through mostly-up normals, so roadside grass glows without blowing out.
- **Shader traps hit**:
  - A geometry without a `normal` attribute makes three compile MeshStandard/Lambert/Phong `FLAT_SHADED` (no `vNormal`). The grass computes normals in the shader, so it carries a dummy normal attribute.
  - `DoubleSide` flips the normal on back faces: foliage cards and grass blades shaded as if facing into the crown / ground (half the grass was black). Foliage uses `NO_FLIP_NORMAL` (`trees.js`).
  - Program variants include the output colour space: `compileAsync` must run with the HDR target bound (`compileScene()` in `main.js`), else it compiles unused sRGB-output programs.
  - Lamp visibility / shadow flags change the lights hash and recompile every lit program; the load warms up with one real frame with all lamps on (first lamp switch at night 600 → 20 ms).
  - Instanced grass is vertex bound on the M1 Pro (~2.5 ms per million blade vertices, culled included). One grid that thins out with distance (`grass.js` header); density, height and width are curves over the distance (`evalCurve`, sampled into `uCurve[65]`), per level in `quality.js` `grassCurve`, tuned by eye on a flat test meadow (Oct 9). Lighting: the normal tilts towards the sun on the seen face and a hot spot lifts the albedo up to 35 % with the sun behind the viewer (before, grass went dark olive with the sun behind). The sun is a `SunLight` (`sunLights[0]`), not in `directionalLights`. `grass.setTiming(renderer, true)` times the grass on the GPU.
- **Mobile preset** (Auto picks it on phones and tablets, `isMobileDevice` incl. iPadOS): no AA, shadows, SSAO; view distance 0.7; Grass and bushes level Off (picking a level makes it Custom). `dynamicDpr` (`updateDynamicResolution` in `main.js`): 12.5 % steps per 2.5 s window, up at ≥ 57 fps, down below 45; a step up landing under 50 fps goes back and that level is blocked for 60 s. Only resizes buffers, never recompiles. `game.updateDynamicResolution(dt, false)` accepts fake frame times.
- **Dust** (`effects.js`): point size in pixels = size (m) × `projectionMatrix[1][1]` × half the render target height / depth, so puffs look the same at any resolution, DPR and fov (a fixed 600 px scale was right only at ~720 px height). At most ~450 puffs (`PUFF_CAP`); wheelspin past 3 m/s adds a quarter as much, so a car stuck spinning makes a low haze, not a wall (it used to fill the 2400 buffer with 4 m puffs).
- **Quality presets** (`quality.js`, applied live). Measured in headless Chrome 1920×1080 @ DPR 2, trail drive, day: Low 5.1 ms (1920×1080, FXAA, no grass), Medium 8.7 (2400×1350, FXAA), High 13.0 (2880×1620, MSAA 2), Ultra 22 (3840×2160, MSAA 4; since Oct 9 Ultra caps pixel density at 150 % like High, so it renders 2880×1620 too); these include AA that presets no longer turn on (all presets default to AA off). The old build measured 11.5–13 ms at 3360×1890 in the same runs (night 13.5–14.1 vs High 13.9–14.4; cockpit 15.1 vs 13.4). Vegetation, whole frame on an M1 Pro: Low 9 ms, Medium 13, High 19, Ultra 46 (Ultra without vegetation 26). Numbers drift ±30 % with other GPU load (other agents' headless pages, the in-app browser): compare interleaved runs, and end CDP runs on `about:blank`.
  - Grass and bushes is one preset row (`gVeg`: off / low…ultra / custom) that works like Shadows: picking a level makes quality Custom and takes that level's grass curves and bush values (`vegOf`); `off` draws neither (`q.vegetation`). It replaced the `vegetation` switch (Oct 8; `Settings` migrates `vegetation: false` to `gVeg: 'off'`); the grass and bush sliders are gone (old saves drop them, a saved Custom level falls back to High). Density is cell spacing (1/√density), so low density really draws fewer instances.
  - Every preset field has a `g`-prefixed setting (`presetToGfx` / `gfxToQuality`); picking a preset writes its values into them (`{ sync: true }`), changing one switches `quality` to `custom`.
- **Sun shadow** (`render/cascadeShadow.js`): three's `SunLightShadow` is fixed at two cascades; this has 2–4 cascades in a 2×2 atlas (High 3 at 2560, Ultra 4 at 4096 = 8192 px atlas, 268 MB; `SHADOWS` in `quality.js`). Four cascade slots are always declared; unused ones aren't drawn (`attach()` makes `getViewportCount()` report only active ones). The terrain has no vertex normal, so a zero `vSunShadowWorldNormal` must not be normalised (NaN made every ground lookup read as lit).
- **Auto quality by measurement** (`updateAutoQuality` in `main.js`, from the rAF loop only, so `game.tick` tests don't trigger it): ladder low → ultra (mobile skips it); start = the preset saved for this GPU + screen size (`offroad.autoQuality.v1`, then only steps down) or the GPU-name guess. < 45 fps → one down; ≥ 57 → one up, which must hold 50 or it goes back and the ceiling stays below. M1 Pro (headless, 1920×1080 @ 2): High holds 60, Ultra ~25 → settles on High. A hidden pane or tab doesn't measure; a throttled but visible pane would measure low fps.
- **SSAO** (`pipeline.js`): flat ground must read exactly 1.0 (check `pipeline.ao[1]` via `readRenderTargetPixels` into a Uint16Array of half floats). That needs depth samples snapped to full-res texel centres, central-difference normals except across depth edges, an occluder threshold of 4 cm + 0.6 % of the distance, and a 4×4 Bayer rotation + one depth-aware 4×4 box blur averaging exactly one period. Without these the ground showed resolution-dependent bands / dot grids (seen on a Mac and a 1440p monitor).
- **Load**: ~3 s to "Ready" (terrain 1.1 s, ground layers/splat/ground data 0.7, props + trees 0.6, compile + warm-up 0.4). `game.loadLog` has stage times.

## Time of day (`world/environment.js`)

- **One number**: the hour, 0..24. Saved `'day' | 'dusk' | 'night'` migrate to 13 / 19.5 / 23. `env.setHour(h, { instant })` is the only entry point (`APPLY.time` in `main.js`): `instant` for the slider, quick buttons and startup, otherwise a 2.5 s sweep *forward* (N key; night to day goes through the whole morning).
- **Key frames** (`KEYS`, wrapping 23:00 → 1:30) between which values follow a periodic monotone cubic (no overshoot, so a key renders exactly its own values). The old presets are keys 13:00 **day**, 19:30 **dusk**, 23:00 **night**, unchanged (`QUICK_HOURS` / `QUICK_ORDER`). To add a look, add a key.
- **Sun and moon follow arcs, not keys** (`SUN_ARC`, `MOON_ARC`). The sun's arc is the best fit through the three presets (13:00 elev 40° az 145°, 19:30 4.5° / 250°, 23:00 −16° / 250°); a key's `pin: [elev, azim]` moves the sun *exactly* onto the preset at that hour (offset computed at load, interpolated with the other key values). That is why the noon peak is 43°, not 50°. Sunrise ~7:00, sunset ~20:00.
- **Continuous, never a mode switch**: direct light = sun + moon (each fades with its own height); shadow direction = the two weighted by brightness; fog / zenith colours = a sun part (kept until the sun is 14° down: twilight) + a moon part. At the three presets only one part is non-zero, so they match the old code (checked by diffing every light, fog, sky and post uniform). The transmittance of a sun below the horizon dip is clamped to the horizon's last dim red value, else it would jump to 0.
- **Exposure**: `auto` is 0..1 (was a switch at 0.5): the adapt shader cross-fades in log space between fixed `exposure` and the eye-adaptation target; the luminance pass runs only while `auto > 0.001`, so by day the frame costs what it did. Exposure resets (no lag) at every `apply`, so the picture follows a slider exactly. Blue-hour and pre-dawn keys run fully adaptive: otherwise the frame fell to a black dip between the lost sun and the moon (all 24 hours checked on a contact sheet).
- **`env.darkness`** (0..1) = 1 − smoothstep(sun elevation, −10°, 4.5°) drives everything that fades (tail / instrument backlight, `uEnvSpec` 1 → 1.6, dust multiplier 1 → 0.12). **`env.night`** (boolean) is the same with hysteresis (on above 0.7 ≈ sun −5°, off below 0.45 ≈ sun −2°), only for decisions: lamp visibility (changes the lights hash) and automatic headlights via `env.onNightChange`. A back-and-forth slider drag flips it only at the two hysteresis points (21 flips in a test drag, programs 92 → 92).
- **Light probe** (PMREM of the sky dome): three's `fromScene()` does the whole GGX prefilter in one frame (35 ms on a 38 ms frame), a hitch every 0.25 s during a slider drag. `updateProbe` runs the same steps one per frame (~15 frames) using PMREMGenerator internals (`_setSize`, `_allocateTargets`, `_sceneToCubeUV`, `_applyGGXFilter`; three is pinned) and falls back to `fromScene` if they're missing. Ambient lags the slider by ≲ 0.3 s. `env.settle()` finishes synchronously (tests, screenshots).
- **Testing**: `game.env.setHour(h, { instant: true })`, then `game.env.settle()`, then tick. Pause (`game.setPaused(true)`; `game.redraw = 1e9`) for a static scene, and turn `autoPause` off (a headless page gets `blur` events and opens the menu). Frame cost is unchanged by the continuous time of day (GPU within ±1 ms of the old presets). The first sunset after load can still hitch (parked in TASKS).
